Finding and comparing trains

A search returns every journey we can build for the date you asked for, direct trains first and connections after. Each result shows the cheapest product still available on that journey. Opening the result shows the full ladder, because the cheapest fare is rarely the one that suits everybody.

How the fare classes differ

Five seat products are sold. Which of them appears depends on the train: First is sold on Acela only, and Business Class is not sold on every long-distance train.

The five seat products, where each one is sold, and how it behaves after purchase

Saver The lowest rung of the ladder, in a limited bucket Not permitted None
Value The everyday fare, sold while seats last Free before departure Travel credit
Flexible Held back, so it is still there close to departure Free at any time Original payment method

What each product includes beyond the reservation itself is the carrier's to describe, not ours, so the conditions attached to the product you have chosen are shown with the fare before you pay.

What a “from” price means

The price on a result row is the total for one adult, in the cheapest product still available on that journey, on that date, including the agency service charge. It is a starting point, not a quotation. Four things move it: how many people are travelling, which passenger types they are, which product you choose, and, on a connection, the fact that the journey is priced as the sum of its legs rather than as a single through fare. The full breakdown, with the service charge as its own line, appears before you pay. See fares and refunds for how that charge is set.

Why Saver disappears as the date approaches

Saver is a small bucket rather than a discount. When the seats allocated to it on a given train are gone, the fare is gone, and the next product up becomes the cheapest thing on the row. In this demonstration Saver is withheld entirely inside ten days of departure, and on a proportion of departures further out it is already sold out. That is the behaviour a real yield-managed fare ladder has, so a price that rises between two searches on different days is not an error.

How far ahead you can book

We apply Amtrak's published booking limits: 335 days ahead for a single journey and 90 days for a multi-ride, a maximum of five segments in one multi-city itinerary, and online booking closing 15 minutes before the train departs.

Seats, rooms and how each one is priced

A reservation and an accommodation are different products, and the difference shows up in the total before it shows up anywhere else. A seat is priced per person. A room is priced per room. Two people sharing a room pay for that room once, not twice.

Because a room is charged once, passenger-type discounts do not apply to it. A senior or child discount reduces a seat fare; it does not reduce the price of a room, since the room costs the same whoever is inside it. On an overnight train the party still pays the room price and nothing per head.

Accommodations include meals on board. That is part of why a room price set against a seat fare looks steep: it covers the room and the dining that goes with it, for everyone in the room, rather than a seat with more space around it.

Four accommodations are named in the fare set: Roomette, Bedroom, Family Room and Accessible Bedroom. All four are priced per room and all four include meals on board. Family Rooms exist on Superliner equipment only, so they do not appear on every long-distance train. We do not describe the inside of each room here, because the layout depends on the equipment rostered for the train and that is the carrier's to state: what the room you have chosen holds is shown with the fare conditions before you pay.

There is a third unit of sale. On the Auto Train this demonstration sells the vehicle as its own product, priced per vehicle rather than per person, alongside the seat or room the people travel in.

Rooms are the scarcest thing we sell. The room counts generated for a departure are small, so a journey that shows seats freely may show no room at all.

Connections and minimum connection times

A connection is two or three separate trains sold together on one booking. We build itineraries with up to two changes. Each leg is a real scheduled service in Amtrak's published timetable, and some legs are Amtrak Thruway Connecting Services, which are scheduled motorcoach runs that appear inside a rail itinerary rather than a separate booking you have to make yourself.

We will not offer a change unless the gap between the two trains meets our minimum connection time. These are our figures, not the carrier's, and they are deliberately generous: a missed connection onto a long-distance train that runs once a day costs a day.

Minimum time we require between an arrival and the next departure
Where you change Minimum Why
Standard station 45 min Enough to walk between platforms and absorb a short delay
Busy station 60 min Applied at the stations with the most calls in the network, where a change means more walking and more people to walk through
Long-distance train involved 90 min Applies when either train is a long-distance service or the Auto Train, whichever side of the change it is on

The longest of the applicable rules wins. Changing onto a long-distance train at a busy station needs 90 minutes, not 60.

At the other end, we cap the waiting. An itinerary with a gap of more than eight hours is not offered, unless it is the only way to make the journey that day, in which case we will go as far as fifteen hours rather than show you nothing.

What “tight connection” means on a result

A change is flagged as tight when the gap is under 50 minutes. It is legal by our own rules and we will sell it, but it leaves very little room: if the first train runs late, there is no second option built into the itinerary. The flag is there so the choice is yours rather than ours. If the day allows it, the same search will usually also show a longer connection for a similar time of arrival.

One more thing worth knowing before you pick a connection: only products that every leg can carry are sold end to end. If one leg has no Business Class, Business Class will not appear on that itinerary even though the other train has it.

Changes, refunds and travel credit

What you can do after booking is decided by the fare class you bought, and by nothing else. We charge no change fee of our own on any product. A change is re-priced at the fare available at the moment you make it, so if the cheaper fare has gone, the difference is payable.

Travel credit means the value is held against your booking reference and can be put towards a later booking with us, rather than returned to the card you paid with. Original payment method means the money goes back the way it came.

Two limits apply whatever you bought. Changes made online stop 15 minutes before the train departs, the same cut-off as booking. And on a connecting itinerary a change affects the whole journey, because the legs are held together by the connection rules above: moving one train may leave the next connection below its minimum, in which case the itinerary has to be rebuilt rather than edited.

Bookings are managed under my trips, using the reservation number and the name on the booking.

Luggage, pets and bicycles

These are carrier rules, not ours, and we are careful about the difference. We do not publish a baggage allowance of our own and we do not restate the carrier's here, because what may be carried on, what may be checked and which stations can handle checked bags differ between trains and change without reference to us. The conditions that apply to the train you have chosen are shown with the fare conditions before you pay.

Pets

Amtrak's published pet rules are worth reading before you book rather than after, because several of them rule a journey out entirely:

  • Dogs and cats only, and they must travel in a carrier.
  • A 20-pound limit for the pet and carrier combined.
  • Trips of up to 7 hours on most routes.
  • One pet per customer, with a fee charged per segment.
  • The carrier counts as one piece of your carry-on allowance.
  • Not available on the Auto Train, and not into Canada on the Adirondack, Maple Leaf or Cascades.

Pet travel is not sold as a product in this demonstration. The rules are reproduced so you can tell whether a journey qualifies; in a live deployment the pet space is added in the booking flow where the distribution feed carries it.

Bicycles

This demonstration sells no bicycle space, because no such product exists in the demo fare set. In a live deployment a bicycle space is offered only where the distribution feed carries it as something bookable; where it cannot be reserved through us, it appears as a condition to read rather than an item to buy.

Children and young travellers

Passenger types are chosen before the search, because they change both the price and what can be booked at all. The discounts attached to them are the agency's own retail proposition and are configured per storefront, so they are not a restatement of the carrier's.

  • Infant, under 2, travelling on an adult's lap.
  • Child, 2 to 12.
  • Youth, 13 to 15.
  • Adult, 16 and over.
  • Senior, 65 and over. Amtrak states that travellers 65 and over are eligible for 10% off most rail fares on most trains, and that on services operated jointly with VIA Rail Canada the age is 60. Proof of age is required.
  • Military, serving or veteran, with ID required at boarding.

Three of Amtrak's booking rules bite most often on family bookings. A reservation may contain a maximum of four different passenger types. One online transaction may carry a maximum of eight fare-paying passengers, with 9 to 14 handled by telephone and 15 or more through a group request. And passengers aged 13, 14 and 15 travelling alone cannot be booked online, so an unaccompanied young traveller has to be arranged with us rather than put through a search result.

If you are travelling overnight with children, compare the Family Room against separate seats before deciding. It is charged once for the room and includes meals for everyone in it, but it exists on Superliner equipment only.

Travelling with a disability

Assistance is requested inside the booking flow, at the point where you name the travellers. There is no separate telephone line to call afterwards and no form to send. The request is recorded against the individual traveller and against each station independently, so a journey between a station that offers assistance and one that does not still captures the request at the station that does, and a connection captures it at the change as well. Whatever is recorded is carried on the itinerary rather than living in a separate system.

We built it that way because the alternative has been measured. Amtrak's own Office of Inspector General tested online booking for its audit OIG-A-2025-009, Train Operations: The Company Can Improve the Quality of Customer Service to Passengers with Disabilities, published 11 July 2025, and reported: “When we booked trips online between a station that offered assistance and one that did not, we could not request assistance at either station.” Separately, Amtrak's own booking configuration directs anyone reserving for more than one traveller with a disability to telephone instead, and notes that “A new experience for multiple traveler bookings is in progress.”

The Accessible Bedroom is sold like any other room: priced per room, with meals on board included. In this demonstration it is held back for travellers who need it rather than offered as a general upgrade.

Two honest limits. In this demonstration an assistance request is stored with the booking and shown back to you, but nothing is transmitted to a carrier, because nothing here is transmitted to a carrier at all. In a live deployment whether the request reaches the train depends on what the distribution agreement carries, and that is stated per deployment rather than assumed. The interface itself is built to WCAG 2.1 Level AA, and the accessibility statement sets out what that covers and where it falls short.

Boarding and your eTicket

Bring the eTicket and, where a discounted passenger type was used, the proof that goes with it: proof of age for a senior fare, ID for a military fare. The reservation number is six alphanumeric characters and is the one thing worth writing down, since every other document can be found again from it.

The eTicket carries a QR code (ISO/IEC 18004). It is an index into the reservation rather than a copy of it: a decoded example held the reservation number and the purchase date and nothing more. That is also why the eTicket shows no price. Fare and payment details sit on a separate sales receipt, along with the 13-digit ticket number, which never appears on the ticket itself.

Printing works, and the page is styled for it. Two things to respect if you print: a QR code needs a clear margin of four modules on all four sides to scan reliably, so do not crop it or shrink the ticket to fit alongside something else; and print at actual size rather than scaled to page. A phone screen at reasonable brightness is read just as easily.

Booking and online changes both close 15 minutes before departure. After that, everything happens at the station.

This is a demonstration

This storefront is a working demonstration of a white-label rail retailing platform. It is run by an independent retailer. It is not Amtrak, is not affiliated with Amtrak, and the name is used only to identify the services being described.

The schedules are real. They are compiled from Amtrak's published GTFS feed: 646 stops, 61 routes and 2,992 trips, with a service calendar running to 2 August 2027. We corrected 12 timezone defects in that feed before using it — seven Arizona stations were labelled America/Denver although Arizona does not observe daylight saving, and five Gulf Coast stations were labelled America/New_York when Mississippi and Alabama are Central. Five of those also had their stop times written in local time rather than the feed's Eastern convention. Amtrak publishes no GTFS-Realtime feed, so nothing here shows a train running late.

Everything transactional is simulated. Amtrak publishes no public fare or booking API, so the fares, the seat and room counts, the payment step and the tickets are all generated by this build. Live inventory reaches a reseller only through a distribution agreement: with Amtrak directly, through the agent portal now operated by Distribusion Technologies, or through an aggregator such as SilverRail. The fare engine is written as a swappable provider for exactly that reason, which how it connects explains.

Nothing booked here is a reservation and no ticket produced here is valid for travel. Amtrak's ticket terms are explicit on the point: Amtrak may refuse to carry passengers “Who present an Amtrak ticket purchased from an unauthorized third party. Amtrak tickets may only be sold or issued by Amtrak or an authorized travel agent/tour operator. Any ticket purchased from an unauthorized third party will be voided. The ticket holder will not be eligible for travel or for a refund.” Our demonstration tickets are marked as specimens so they cannot be mistaken for the real thing, following the same principle as the European rail standard for test tickets, which sets a specimen flag inside the barcode data and holds that “tickets signed using a test key can never be considered as valid”.
