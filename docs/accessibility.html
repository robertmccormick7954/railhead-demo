Working standard: WCAG 2.1 Level AA

Accessibility

Two different things are covered here, and they are kept apart on purpose. The first is whether this website can be operated by everyone who needs to use it. The second is what happens to a passenger who needs assistance at a station or on board.

This website

The target is WCAG 2.1 Level AA. It was the standard the interface was designed against rather than a checklist run over it afterwards, which is why the station pickers are a combobox rather than a styled list of clickable boxes, and why the departure board carries a second, plain version of itself for screen readers.

Accessibility work already in this build
Area What is in place
Keyboard Everything that can be clicked is a real link, button or form field, so the whole site is operable from the keyboard alone. Nothing is driven by a click handler on a plain container.
Focus A three-pixel focus outline, offset from the control so it stays legible against a filled button. It is drawn for keyboard focus only, so it does not flash on a mouse click.
Skip link The first item in the tab order on every page. It is hidden until it takes focus, then moves focus past the header and the tenant strip to the main landmark.
Station pickers An ARIA combobox with a listbox of matches. Typing filters on station name, city, state and three-letter code, so penn, new york and NYP all reach the same station. Arrow keys move through the matches, Enter takes the highlighted one, and Escape closes the list without discarding what was typed.
Form errors An error is written next to the field it belongs to, the field is marked invalid, and the two are tied together with aria-describedby so the message is read out when the field takes focus. Confirmations are announced through a polite live region rather than a pop-up that steals focus.
Contrast Checked programmatically rather than by eye, in both colour schemes and for every tenant palette. Translucent surfaces are composited against whatever sits behind them before the ratio is measured, because that is the colour a reader actually sees.
Reduced motion When reduced motion is requested, the departure board does not run its split-flap animation and site-wide transitions are cut. Separately, and regardless of that setting, the animated grid of characters is hidden from assistive technology and an equivalent plain text list of the same departures is exposed instead.
Structure One first-level heading per page and no skipped levels below it, landmarks with accessible names, and tables with a caption and scoped header cells. Colour is never the only thing carrying a meaning.
Print A dedicated print stylesheet. Navigation, the departure board and the footer are dropped, a ticket is kept to a single sheet, and the barcode keeps a module size that survives a home printer.

What this build has not had

This is a demonstration build. It has not had an independent accessibility audit, and it has not been tested by people who use assistive technology daily. The checks behind the table above were run by the people who wrote the code, using automated tooling and manual keyboard and screen-reader passes. That combination finds real defects and reliably misses others, particularly around wording, expectations and anything that depends on how a specific assistive technology behaves in practice.

Nothing here is a claim of conformance or certification. No conformance report has been published for this build, and none should be inferred from the standard named at the top of this page.

Reporting a barrier

If something on this site stopped you, tell us through contact. The three things that make a report actionable are the address of the page, what you were trying to do at the point it failed, and the browser and assistive technology you were using, with versions if you have them. A barrier report is treated as a defect in the build rather than a support request. Because this is a demonstration, there is no response-time commitment attached to it.

Travelling with a disability

Two things about booking assisted travel through Amtrak's own channels are on the public record. They are set out below because they are the requirement this platform was built to meet, not as a comment on the carrier.

Amtrak booking configuration

Amtrak's configuration tells a customer reserving for more than one traveller with a disability to call the carrier by telephone, and adds that “A new experience for multiple traveler bookings is in progress.”

Published in Amtrak's own booking-widget configuration. The telephone number it gives is not reproduced on this page.

Audit OIG-A-2025-009

“When we booked trips online between a station that offered assistance and one that did not, we could not request assistance at either station.”

Amtrak Office of Inspector General, Train Operations: The Company Can Improve the Quality of Customer Service to Passengers with Disabilities, 11 July 2025. The finding describes the auditors' own tests of the website and app.

What this platform does instead

More than one traveller with a disability can be booked in a single transaction. There is no point in the flow where the itinerary has to be split in two, abandoned for a phone call, or rebuilt from scratch because of who is travelling.

Station assistance is requested inside the booking flow, on the same step where the passengers are entered, and it is requested for a named passenger at a named station rather than as a note attached to the whole trip. Once made, the request is carried on the booking record: it stays with the reservation, and it is shown again when the booking is retrieved or the itinerary is reprinted, so nobody has to remember whether it was asked for.

An Accessible Bedroom is one of the sleeping accommodations offered on services that carry them. Like the other accommodations it is priced and sold as a room rather than per person, and meals on board are included.

What we do not decide

The assistance request is passed to the carrier with the booking. What is actually available is governed by the carrier's own rules: whether a given station offers assistance at all, which platforms and entrances can be used, what notice period applies, and what the equipment on a particular train can do. This platform can carry the request accurately and make sure it is not lost. It cannot create a service at a station that does not have one, and it will not tell you that a station is staffed when the carrier says otherwise.

This is a demonstration. The schedules are real, taken from Amtrak's published GTFS feed. Everything transactional is simulated: no booking made here reaches Amtrak, no assistance request is transmitted to anyone, and no ticket issued here is valid for travel. To arrange assistance for a real journey, contact the carrier or the travel agency that sold the ticket.
