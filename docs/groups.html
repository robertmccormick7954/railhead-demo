Parties of nine or more

Group travel

Eight is the number that decides everything on this page. Amtrak's online booking takes up to eight fare-paying passengers in one transaction, and the ninth traveller ends the online route entirely. A coach party, a wedding, a works outing and a school trip all stop in exactly the same place, and the usual answer is to put the whole thing on the telephone.

Where the online booking stops

These are the carrier's published limits on its own online booking. They are not ours, we cannot lift them, and no retailer can.

Amtrak's published online booking limits and how each one lands on a group
Limit The published rule What it means for a group
Passengers in one online transaction Maximum 8 fare-paying passengers Nothing about the journey changes at nine people. Only the way it has to be booked does.
Parties of 9 to 14 Sent to a telephone line Someone has to read out every name, date and station, and write down the answer. That is the call this form replaces.
Parties of 15 or more Sent to a group request form Treated as a formal group request rather than an ordinary transaction, and answered by people rather than by a results page.
Passenger types in one reservation Maximum 4 different types This storefront offers six passenger types, so any four of them fit inside one reservation; a fifth does not, and the party has to be split.
Booking horizon 335 days ahead, or 90 days for multi-ride products A date further out than that is not open to anyone yet, so it cannot be priced or held by us or by the carrier.

Two smaller limits catch groups with connections. Online booking closes 15 minutes before departure, which rules out assembling a large party at the last moment. And an online multi-city itinerary holds at most five segments with the carrier; this storefront searches trips of up to eight legs, but the reservation behind them still has to respect the carrier's structure.

The passenger-type limit is the one people meet without expecting it. Four types is enough for most families and not enough for most organised parties.

What we do instead of the phone call

A group enquiry is a list of facts. Written down once, in fields with names, it can be read, checked, quoted and repeated without anybody hearing a station name wrongly. Spoken down a line it has to be captured live by someone typing while you talk. The form on this page is the same conversation, in the order we would have asked for it.

What we need from you

Six things carry almost all of the weight, and a group enquiry that arrives without them turns into a second conversation before anyone can look anything up.

  • The head count, and the breakdown behind it. Twenty travellers is not one fact. It is some number of adults, some children, possibly a senior or two, and each of those is a passenger type that has to fit inside the carrier's limit of four per reservation.
  • Both stations, in the direction you are travelling. Station names repeat across the network, so the pair settles which journey is meant.
  • The outbound date, and whether you are coming back. A return leg is a second set of space to find, not a detail added at the end.
  • How flexible the date is. A large party rarely fits the first service you looked at. A day either way is often the difference between one reservation and three, and we would rather know that at the start than propose something that cannot work.
  • Accessibility requirements, in words. These are asked for early on this page and there is a reason for it, set out with the form below.
  • One contact person. Groups fall apart on this. One name, one email, one telephone number, and a group name so the enquiry can be found again.

We do not need a passenger list to start. Names come later, once there is something to put them on.

What happens after you send it

We read the enquiry, check the timetable for services that can take the party together, and put the request to the carrier. Response times belong to the agency running this storefront and are published by that agency; none is promised on this page. If something in the enquiry is missing or contradicts itself, we come back and ask rather than guess.

When the carrier answers, we come back with what is actually available, the conditions attached to it, and what has to be paid and by when. The terms on a group booking may differ from those on an ordinary one, and we set out whatever applies in writing before anything is due.

What we cannot promise

Group space is confirmed by the carrier, not by us and not by this page. Sending the form reserves nothing, holds nothing and prices nothing. Until the carrier confirms, everything we quote is a quotation, and a quotation can be gone by the time it is accepted.

Nor can we route around the carrier. Amtrak's ticket terms state that "Amtrak tickets may only be sold or issued by Amtrak or an authorized travel agent/tour operator", and that a ticket bought from an unauthorised third party will be voided, with the holder eligible neither for travel nor for a refund. A group is exactly the situation in which somebody offers to sell twenty tickets cheaply. That is the rule to check them against.

This storefront is a demonstration. The schedules it searches are real and come from Amtrak's published GTFS feed, which contains no fare data of any kind. Everything transactional here is simulated: no enquiry is sent anywhere, no space is held, and no ticket can be issued.

School, youth and unaccompanied young travellers

School parties meet the four-type limit immediately. Children aged 2 to 12, youth aged 13 to 15, adult staff and one chaperone aged 65 or over already fill it, and an infant on a lap makes a fifth type. The party then cannot sit in one reservation. That is not a problem, but it has to be planned rather than discovered at payment: tell us the ages you are bringing and we split the party deliberately across reservations on the same service.

There is one rule that no form can work around. Passengers aged 13, 14 and 15 travelling alone cannot be booked online with the carrier. For an accompanied school trip this rarely matters on the outbound leg and often matters on the way home, when a pupil leaves the group early or joins it late. An unaccompanied young traveller has to be arranged with a person, which is a genuine reason to write to us rather than search for a fare.

If your party includes young travellers who will at any point be on a train without an accompanying adult, say so in the notes. It changes what can be booked, not just what it costs.

Accessibility requirements

This is asked here, near the top, because the carrier's own online booking handles it poorly. Amtrak's booking configuration tells anyone making a reservation for more than one traveller with a disability to telephone the carrier instead, and adds that "A new experience for multiple traveler bookings is in progress". An Amtrak Office of Inspector General audit published on 11 July 2025, Train Operations: The Company Can Improve the Quality of Customer Service to Passengers with Disabilities (OIG-A-2025-009), tested booking on the carrier's website and app and found: "When we booked trips online between a station that offered assistance and one that did not, we could not request assistance at either station." Written into an enquiry, a requirement can be raised by name, station and date. Left to a checkbox on a results page, it can be lost.

Wheelchair spaces and how many, station assistance at either end, a travelling companion, a service animal, seating that has to be together, an accessible bedroom on an overnight service, or anything else that would make the difference between a workable itinerary and one that only looks workable. Accommodation and assistance are confirmed by the carrier, so the earlier it is asked for, the better the chance of getting it.

Anything else

What the trip is for, whether the party must travel on one service or can be split, luggage or equipment beyond normal baggage, any traveller aged 13 to 15 who will be on a train without an accompanying adult, and anything that is fixed for a reason, such as an event start time.
