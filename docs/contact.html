There are three ways to reach a person: the telephone, email, and the form on this page. Which one is right depends mostly on how soon you travel. The telephone number, the email address and the staffed hours belong to the agency running this storefront, and the footer of every page carries them rather than this page repeating them.

Choosing a channel

Support channels and what each one is for
Channel Use it for How soon
Telephone Anything time-critical: a train today or tomorrow, a missed connection, a booking you need changed before departure. Also the right channel if a written answer would take longer to compose than to speak. While the line is staffed. The hours are in the footer.
Email Anything with a document attached, anything you want a written record of, and billing queries where a receipt has to be read alongside the booking. Slower than the telephone. The agency running this storefront publishes its own times.
This form The same things as email, when you would rather not open a mail client. In a live deployment it joins the same written queue; in this demonstration build it transmits nothing. As email, once a deployment has been wired to a destination.
Your account Retrieving an itinerary, reprinting a ticket, checking what a fare rule allows before you ask anyone. My trips answers most of this without a queue. Immediately.

No response time is promised here. Each agency operating on this platform sets and publishes its own, and this page cannot state one on their behalf. The ordering holds either way: the telephone answers sooner than anything written. If you travel within the next two days, ring rather than write.

Three things are better raised elsewhere. The carrier's own online booking stops at eight passengers, and a party of fifteen or more is handled through a group request rather than a booking screen, so start at group travel. Something on this website that blocked you — a control you could not operate, a page a screen reader could not follow — is a defect in the build, and the accessibility page explains what to include in the report. Questions about what a fare class permits are answered on fares and refunds, which is faster than asking.

What to have ready

Nobody can answer a question about a booking until they can find the booking. Four details make that immediate.

  • The reservation number. Six alphanumeric characters. It is on the confirmation and on the ticket itself, and it is the fastest way to locate a booking.
  • The date of travel and the two stations, in the direction you are travelling. Station names repeat across the network, so the date settles which journey is meant.
  • The passenger name exactly as it was entered on the booking, including middle names or initials if they were given. A booking is found under the name it carries, not the name it should have carried.
  • For a billing question, the ticket number, thirteen digits. It appears on the sales receipt rather than on the ticket, because an Amtrak eTicket shows no price at all. Payment details live on the receipt document.

Send the reservation number as text. A screenshot of it cannot be searched for.
