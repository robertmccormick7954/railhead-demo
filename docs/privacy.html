Privacy

What this demonstration stores, and where

This storefront is a demonstration. It runs as static files with no application server behind it: no account, no sign-in, no database of ours. The schedules are real, drawn from Amtrak's published GTFS feed, and everything transactional is simulated. Whatever you do here stays on the device you are reading this on.

What is kept in your browser

The demo writes six items into your browser's own storage. Five persist until you remove them; one is discarded by the browser when you close the tab. Nothing else is written, and none of it is transmitted.

Browser storage written by this build
Key Store What it holds, and why
railhead.tenant localStorage Which of the demo storefronts you last chose, so the site opens on the same one instead of resetting.
railhead.scheme localStorage Whether you asked for the light or dark appearance, or left it to follow your system setting.
railhead.brandcache localStorage The chosen storefront's colours, so a page can paint them immediately on the next visit rather than flashing one brand and then repainting in another.
railhead.bookings localStorage The simulated bookings you have made here, so that managing a booking has something to show.
railhead.override.<storefront> localStorage Any change you made in the agency console — colours, markup, payment methods, support details. It is written once per storefront you edit, and removing it restores that storefront to its supplied configuration.
railhead.basket sessionStorage The search and the selection you are part-way through, so a reload does not lose them. The browser drops it when the tab closes.

No identifier is generated for you, and nothing is derived about your device beyond the two preferences above. If you type a passenger name into the booking flow, it is written into railhead.bookings on your machine and goes nowhere else. Because this is a demonstration and no ticket can be issued from it, there is no reason to enter real personal details, and we would rather you did not.

You can read all of it yourself. Open your browser's developer tools, go to the storage or application panel, and look under this site's origin. What is listed there is the whole of it.

Removing it

Clearing this site's data deletes all five keys. In most browsers the route is settings, then the privacy or history section, then clearing browsing data with "cookies and other site data" selected; every current browser also lets you clear data for a single site from the padlock or site-information button in the address bar. In developer tools, the storage panel has a delete control per key and a clear-all for the origin.

Afterwards the demo forgets which storefront you picked, returns to following your system's light or dark setting, and shows no saved bookings. Nothing is recoverable, because no copy exists anywhere else. Browsing in a private or incognito window has the same effect on closing: the storage is created and then discarded with the session.

What this page loads, and from whom

Every request a page here makes is to this same site: the HTML, one stylesheet set, the JavaScript modules, and four data files holding the storefront configurations, the station list, the network and the feed metadata. There is no analytics, no tag manager, no pixel, no session recorder, no A/B tool, no chat widget, no advertising script, no embedded map or video, and no social button. There is no third party at all, so there is nothing to consent to and no consent banner.

The build sets no cookies. The browser storage described above exists only to do the thing you asked for by using the page, and it is readable and removable by you at any time.

Fonts are served from here, not from a font host

The three typefaces are hosted on this origin as WOFF2 files rather than requested from a font service. A font hosted elsewhere would mean a request to that company from every visitor's browser on every page, disclosing the visitor's IP address, user agent and the page they were on, before anyone had asked them anything. Railhead is white-labelled, so that disclosure would be made on behalf of an agency, out of its own customers' browsers, about people who had never heard of the font host. Self-hosting removes the request instead of explaining it.

The one thing that is not in our hands

Whichever web server or content delivery network serves these files will keep its own access logs, as web servers do: address, timestamp, path requested, user agent. That belongs to the host, not to this build, and nothing in the demo adds to those logs or reads them.

What a production deployment would add

Selling real tickets changes the picture completely. The four things below are unavoidable in a live deployment, and each of them puts personal data somewhere other than the traveller's own browser. None of them exists here.

A payment processor

Card details would be collected by the processor's own fields and would go to the processor, which acts as an independent controller for its part of the work under its own privacy notice, not as a supplier hidden behind the retailer's. A retailer that lets card numbers touch its own systems takes on the full weight of PCI DSS, which is the reason almost nobody does.

The carrier

A ticket only exists once the carrier's reservation system has it. Issuing one means passing the passenger's name and the details of the journey to the carrier, which then holds them for carriage, changes, refunds and its own legal obligations. Nothing beyond what the ticket needs has to travel with it.

An email provider

Confirmations, itinerary changes and receipts leave through a mail provider, which necessarily handles the recipient's address and the contents of the message, and usually keeps delivery records.

Server-side records

Accounts, orders, payment references and support correspondence would live in a database rather than in browser storage, with application logs, backups and error reports beside them. Each of those needs a stated retention period, and backups need one of their own.

A tenant taking Railhead live must publish, in its own name: who the controller is and how to reach it, the categories of personal data it collects and the purpose of each, the lawful basis for each purpose, who receives the data including the carrier and the payment processor, any transfer outside the country of collection, how long each category is kept, how someone exercises their rights, and which supervisory authority takes complaints. Those answers depend on the entity and where it trades, so this page cannot pre-empt them.

This demonstration does none of the above. It takes no payment, sends no email, contacts no carrier and keeps no server-side record, because it issues nothing.

Rights over data held about you

Where an operator holds personal data about someone, data protection law generally gives that person the right to be told whether data about them is held and to obtain a copy; to have inaccurate data corrected; to have data erased when it is no longer needed for the purpose it was collected for; to have processing restricted while a dispute is resolved; to receive data they provided in a portable form; to object to processing carried out on the basis of a legitimate interest, and to direct marketing without qualification; to withdraw consent where consent was the basis; and to complain to a data protection authority.

Which of those apply, and in what form, depends on the law covering the operator and the traveller, and the responsible party is the agency running the storefront, not the platform it is built on. A live tenant names itself, states its lawful basis for each purpose and gives a working route for these requests. Note that requests can be limited by an operator's other obligations: transaction and tax records commonly have to be kept for a fixed number of years, which restricts erasure while that period runs.

In this demonstration there is nothing to make such a request against. No record about you is held, so there is nothing to disclose, correct, port or delete on our side. The five browser keys listed above are already in your possession, and clearing site data is the erasure.

This page is not a notice for a live storefront

What is written here is accurate for the demonstration build and only for it. An agency putting Railhead into service must replace this page with its own notice, naming its legal entity, its registered address and its data protection contact, and describing the processing it actually carries out. Leaving this text in place on a trading storefront would be untrue from the moment the first payment is taken. If you have a question about this page, use the contact page.
