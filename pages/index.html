Why this exists

Four limits we removed

Each of these is a published limit of the carrier's own retail website, not a guess about it. A booking made through a distribution channel is not bound by the caps the carrier applies to its own online shop.

Party size

More than eight travellers

The carrier's website takes eight fare-paying passengers per transaction and sends nine to fourteen to a telephone line. Here they stay on one reservation.

Accessibility

Two travellers with disabilities

The carrier's own booking configuration reads: “To make reservations for more than one traveler with a disability, call 1-800-USA-RAIL.” This booking flow does it online.

Assistance

Station assistance, in the flow

A federal audit found the carrier's own testers could not request station assistance while booking online. Here it is a step in the booking, carried on the record.

Itineraries

Eight legs, not five

Open-jaw and multi-stop trips that exceed the carrier's five-segment online cap are built and priced as one itinerary.

Under the bonnet

Where the timetable comes from

Every departure, arrival, calling point and connection on this site is read from the carrier's own published GTFS feed. Nothing about the schedule is invented, and nothing is scraped: the feed is public, and it is the only Amtrak data source that is.

Fares are a different matter. The feed contains no fare data at all, and there is no public fare API, so prices here are generated by a clearly-labelled simulation behind a swappable provider interface. A production deployment prices through a contracted distribution feed.
