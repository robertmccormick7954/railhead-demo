Product reference

Fares and refunds

Two products are sold on a train: a place for a person, and a room for a party. They are priced differently, they carry different conditions after purchase, and the total on the breakdown is easier to read once that difference is clear. This page sets out both, then explains what our own charge is and what happens when a booking is cancelled.

Seat fare classes

Five seat products are sold: Saver, Value, Flexible, Business and First. A seat is priced per passenger, and the fare class decides what may be done with the booking afterwards. Which classes appear on a given journey depends on the train rather than on the price you are willing to pay. First is sold on Acela only, and Business Class is not sold on every long-distance train. On a connecting itinerary, only a class that every leg can carry is offered end to end.

We charge no change fee of our own on any class. A change is re-priced at the fare available at the moment it is made, so if the class you originally bought has gone, the difference is payable. Changes made online close 15 minutes before the train departs, which is the same cut-off as booking.

Passenger-type discounts reduce a seat fare. Amtrak states that travellers aged 65 and over are eligible for 10% off most rail fares on most trains, and that on services operated jointly with VIA Rail Canada the age is 60. Proof of age is required, and selecting a discounted passenger type is a statement that the passenger qualifies.

Sleeping accommodations

Four accommodations are sold: Roomette, Bedroom, Family Room and Accessible Bedroom. All four are priced per room, not per person. Two people sharing a Roomette pay for the Roomette once. Because the room costs the same whoever is inside it, passenger-type discounts do not reduce it, and a party of adults and children pays the room price and nothing per head.

A room is booked in addition to the travel of the people in it. It is not sold to anyone who is not on that train: every occupant is a named passenger on the same booking, on the same journey, and the room is added to that party once. That is why a room appears on the breakdown as a single line while seats appear one per passenger.

Accommodations include meals on board. That is part of why a room price set against a coach fare looks steep at first reading. It is a bed and the dining that goes with it, for everyone in the room, rather than a seat with more space around it.

Rooms are the scarcest thing we sell. This demonstration offers only a handful of each type on a train, so a journey showing coach seats freely may show no room at all. All four accommodations may be changed free of charge before departure, and a cancellation returns travel credit rather than money to the card.

The Auto Train adds a third unit of sale. A vehicle is booked as its own product, priced per vehicle, alongside the seat or room the people travel in, and one vehicle may be carried per booking.

How our pricing is presented

We are an independent retailer. We are not the carrier, and the money you pay is not all going to the same place, so the breakdown says so.

Every total is built from two kinds of line. The carrier's fare is what the journey costs: one line per passenger for seats, one line per room for accommodations, with any passenger-type discount shown against the line it reduces. The agency service charge is our own, on its own labelled line, and it pays for arranging the sale rather than for the journey. Underneath sits the total. That total is what you pay: there is no charge introduced after it, and nothing compulsory is added at the payment step.

How the charge is calculated

The service charge is a percentage of the carrier's fare for the booking, set by the agency operating the storefront rather than negotiated per sale. The rate can differ by type of service, and it can be held between a floor and a cap so that a very short trip and a very long one both stay sensible. The percentage that was applied is printed on the breakdown next to the amount, so the arithmetic can be checked rather than taken on trust.

Where the charge already appears

The price on a search result is the total for one adult in the cheapest class still available on that journey and date, and it already includes the service charge. Nothing is disclosed late. Opening a result shows the whole ladder, and the full split appears before you confirm anything and again on the sales receipt.

Why it is shown split rather than rolled in

Rolling the charge into the fare would be simpler to read and worse in four specific ways, each of which turns up in practice.

  • The two lines behave differently on a refund. Whether the fare comes back is decided by the carrier's fare conditions. What happens to our charge is decided by us. Presented as one number, there is no honest way to explain a partial refund.
  • You can compare like with like. The carrier's fare is visible as the carrier's fare, so our charge can be judged as what it is, which is the price of buying through us.
  • It is auditable. A percentage against a stated base can be recalculated by anyone holding the breakdown, including the carrier and the agency's own finance team. A single blended figure cannot.
  • Currency conversion has to land somewhere visible. Where an agency sells in a currency other than the carrier's, the fare and the charge are converted as separate amounts rather than merged into one unexplained number.

The same rule holds in the code as on the screen. The fare provider returns the carrier's net price and nothing else, and the service charge is applied afterwards by the pricing policy, so the two are never conflated before they reach you.

Refunds and travel credit

What you get back is decided by the class you bought, and by nothing else. We cannot waive, vary or override the carrier's fare conditions, and we cannot obtain a refund that the fare does not allow. The discretion we do hold is over our own service charge.

A refund to the original payment method returns the money the way it came, in the currency we charged. Travel credit holds the value against the carrier's record of your booking instead. Credit is the carrier's, not ours: its expiry, whether it can be transferred, and what it may be used for are governed by the carrier's rules. We can tell you what the carrier has told us. We cannot extend it, move it to another person, add to it or turn it into cash.

How to request one

Start under my trips with the reservation number and the name on the booking. The reservation number is six alphanumeric characters and appears on the eTicket. If the booking cannot be retrieved, or the request is not one the tool covers, use contact and quote that reservation number.

Where a refund is due, the money comes from the carrier, so how long it takes is set by the carrier's settlement and your card issuer rather than by us. Our service charge is refunded with the fare when the carrier cancels the service or when we have made the booking wrongly. It is not refunded when you cancel a booking that we issued correctly, because the work it paid for was done. It is never part of a travel credit.

What a retailer can and cannot service

We can service the bookings we issued. A booking made anywhere else, whether directly with the carrier, through another agency or through a different white-label storefront running on this platform, sits on that seller's record and is theirs to change, refund or credit. It is not obstruction and it is not a policy we chose. The reservation is held against the party that made it, and only that party can act on it.

So take the request to whoever sold the ticket. If you are not certain who that was, the confirmation email and the sales receipt both name the seller, and the receipt is the document that carries the fare and payment detail. An Amtrak eTicket shows no price at all, which is why the receipt is the one worth keeping.

Fares in this demonstration

Every price on this site is simulated. It is generated by our own fare engine from the journey itself. It is not an Amtrak fare, it was not retrieved from any carrier system, and it is not a quotation for travel. No booking made here reaches Amtrak, and no ticket issued here is valid for travel.

That is a fact about what is publicly available, not a shortcut taken to save effort. Amtrak publishes no public fare or booking API. Amtrak's Terms of Use prohibit using "any robot, spider, site search/retrieval application or other manual or automatic device or process to retrieve, index, 'data mine' or in any way reproduce or circumvent the navigational structure or presentation of the Site or its contents". There is no lawful open source of live fares to read, so the honest option is to model them and say so on the page where they appear.

The schedules, by contrast, are real

Journeys, trains, calling patterns and times come from Amtrak's official GTFS feed at content.amtrak.com/content/gtfs/GTFS.zip. That feed carries 646 stops, 61 routes and 2,992 trips, with a service calendar running to 2 August 2027, and it contains no fare data of any kind. Amtrak publishes no GTFS-Realtime feed either, so nothing here is live running information. We corrected 12 timezone defects in the feed while loading it: seven Arizona stations were labelled America/Denver although Arizona does not observe daylight saving, and five Gulf Coast stations were labelled America/New_York although Mississippi and Alabama are Central.

What a production deployment does instead

Prices come from a contracted distribution feed. Live inventory reaches a reseller only through a distribution agreement: directly with Amtrak, through the agent portal now operated by Distribusion Technologies, or through an aggregator such as SilverRail. The agreement is what makes the seller an authorised one, and that matters to the passenger as well as to us. Amtrak's ticket Terms and Conditions state that Amtrak may refuse to carry passengers "Who present an Amtrak ticket purchased from an unauthorized third party. Amtrak tickets may only be sold or issued by Amtrak or an authorized travel agent/tour operator. Any ticket purchased from an unauthorized third party will be voided. The ticket holder will not be eligible for travel or for a refund."

The fare engine is built as a provider with a swappable implementation for exactly that reason. This demonstration ships the simulated provider. A production deployment registers one that calls the contracted feed, and everything downstream, from the search results to the breakdown and the ticket, reads the same quote shape either way. The class rules, the per-room pricing and the split between the carrier's fare and the agency's service charge described on this page are the parts that do not change when the source of the numbers does.
